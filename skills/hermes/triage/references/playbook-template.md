# Writing an account playbook

The skill holds the method; the playbook holds what is specific to one account. Keep the
playbook private: it names people, customers' habits and internal routines.

Build it from the account's real history, not from memory. Compare what the teams actually
handled with what the labels and automations claim, because they often differ.

## Sections

1. **Phase in force:** which phase applies, and on which instance.
2. **Teams:** id, name, what belongs there, with signals taken from real messages (words,
   labels, attachment types), plus clear counter-examples.
3. **Ambiguous subjects:** topics that could go to two teams, and who decides.
4. **Noise:** senders and subjects to leave alone, with volumes.
5. **Internal traffic:** groups, contacts and agents that are not customers.
6. **Channel notes:** what each inbox looks like (groups, e-mail, voice).
7. **Known side effects:** automations reacting to team, label or assignee changes.
8. **Labels:** which ones to apply, and which ones belong to humans.
9. **Corrections log:** dated rules taught by admins, with the example that motivated them.

## Gathering the facts

Over the last 60 to 90 days: conversations per inbox, per team and per assignee; share with
no team; labels per team; top e-mail domains and subjects; automation rules; sample opening
messages per team. Aggregate where you can, and do not copy customer data into the playbook.
