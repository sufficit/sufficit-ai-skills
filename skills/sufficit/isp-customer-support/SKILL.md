# ISP customer support

Help an internet subscriber understand the verified situation, the next useful
step and who owns follow-up. Apply this guidance to phone, chat, WhatsApp and
agent training. Answer in the customer's language; adapt the examples rather
than reading them as a rigid script.

For scenario handling, read the relevant section of the
[service manual](references/service-manual.md). For handoff notes, closure and
quality review, use the [record and review guide](references/record-and-review.md).

## Establish the service context

Use available authorized account, outage and ticket information before asking
the customer to repeat it. Request only missing information that affects the
next action. Before disclosing account-specific billing or changing account
information, follow the provider's identity-verification process. A caller ID or
knowledge of a name alone is not verification. Never request passwords, payment
card security codes or authentication codes in the conversation.

Distinguish three sources in your reasoning and records:

- Customer report: symptoms, attempted steps, impact and disputed events.
- Verified evidence: actual tool results, timestamps, network observations and
  ticket entries. An entry verifies what was recorded, not that the event occurred.
- Pending explanation: causes, visit completion or payment status still to verify.

Acknowledge concrete impact without accepting an unverified cause or assigning
blame. Summarize the issue briefly and choose the scenario in the manual.

## Constraints that change the response

- Do not turn a sample conversation's hours, fees, eligibility rules or repair
  window into provider policy. Obtain the current provider policy and actual
  ticket state. If unavailable, state what needs confirmation.
- Distinguish restart/power cycle from factory reset and from a Wi-Fi password
  change. Do not prescribe a factory reset as routine troubleshooting.
- On repeat contact, inspect the original timeline and previous attempts; do not
  restart troubleshooting or the service clock by default. Keep continuity with
  the original case when creating a linked escalation is necessary.
- Separate a repair estimate, a confirmed appointment and the next status update.
  Promise only commitments supported by the responsible team or a workflow you
  can actually execute. An escalation request is not accepted priority or a visit.
- Do not claim a ticket was opened, urgency assigned, invoice sent, payment
  received or appointment booked without a successful authorized operation or
  verified record. Without tools, draft the message and proposed action explicitly.
- This skill does not authorize contacting customers, changing network equipment,
  moving appointments, collecting payments or changing tickets. Execute only
  actions within the user's request and existing permissions.

## Produce a useful outcome

For a live reply, give a concise acknowledgement, the verified status and next
step. Include the responsible team and confirmed update arrangement when known.
Do not dump an internal checklist into the customer response.

For training, show the scenario, recommended action, wording to adapt and the
failure it avoids. For quality review, ground observations in a timestamp or
verified record and name missing evidence. Automated transcripts may contain
wrong words, speaker attribution or timestamps; verify consequential excerpts
against audio when available. Do not infer dishonesty, emotion or intent from
voice, an incomplete transcript or a contested ticket.

## Scope and origin

This package generalizes recurring ISP support situations into original training
examples. It contains no source recordings, subscriber identifiers or verbatim
customer transcripts. The scenarios are not findings about a particular provider
or employee. Company-specific policy and technical runbooks remain authoritative;
this manual supplies no legal deadlines or universal entitlement rules.
