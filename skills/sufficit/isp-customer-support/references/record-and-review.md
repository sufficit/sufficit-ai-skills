# Records, handoffs and coaching

## Minimal internal service note

Adapt to the provider's ticket fields. Use account references already in the
system instead of duplicating full identity documents, passwords or payment data.

```text
Issue and onset:
Customer-reported impact:
Existing ticket and original opening time:
Customer account of events:
Verified observations / record entries, with source and timestamp:
Checks already attempted and results:
Unresolved discrepancy or missing evidence:
Action actually completed, result and linked reference:
Responsible team / accepted owner:
Repair estimate or confirmed appointment, if any:
Next status update arrangement, if confirmed:
Preferred approved contact channel:
Conversation outcome vs service-incident status:
```

Label proposed actions as proposals. Record whether an escalation is requested or
accepted. Preserve a disputed visit or closure entry and append the correction or
review result through the authorized workflow. When reopening or linking a case,
retain the initial timeline and history.

## Quality review based on observable behavior

Evaluate only what the evidence supports. Use “not observable” when account tools,
channel records or audio are missing. Do not turn a transcript omission into a
failed action. A short audio file or long untranscribed interval does not establish
operator misconduct. Verify consequential quotes, speakers and times against the
source audio when available; document uncertainty if it is unavailable.

| Dimension | Evidence to look for | Coaching when supported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuity | Existing case and previous attempts used | Avoid repeat troubleshooting and duplicate tickets |
| Safe diagnosis | Reset distinguished from restart; device context checked | Stop destructive generic reset instructions |
| Evidence accuracy | Customer report separated from system entry | Describe contested events without blame |
| Ownership | Recorded action and responsible team | Replace unsupported “urgent” promises with tracked action |
| Timing | Estimate, appointment and update distinguished | Do not invent deadlines or reset the original timeline |
| Billing delivery | Verified destination, send result and receipt status | Separate sending, receiving and paying |
| Handoff | Issue summary and channel availability | Avoid bot loops and restarting the case |
| Closure | Next step and real case reference given | Separate conversation closure from incident resolution |

A review output should name the observable event, its service impact, the specific
improvement and the evidence needed for unresolved questions. Do not score worker
honesty, infer emotion or build a disciplinary conclusion from these examples.

## Practice scenarios

Use these without live mutations when training or checking a revised skill:

1. A subscriber says a bot told them to reset the router, and now they cannot work.
   The bot transcript is unavailable. Expected: clarify restart vs factory reset,
   avoid another reset, establish recovery path and preserve causal uncertainty.
2. A visit is recorded as customer absent; the customer says nobody came.
   Expected: preserve both accounts, verify address/window and request dispatch
   review, without accusing either party or claiming a new booking.
3. A caller has contacted support repeatedly; the only note says “urgent.”
   Expected: original timeline, missing owner/response identified and an actionable
   escalation; no invented repair time or claim of an executed callback.
4. A former subscriber asks whether paying old invoices guarantees installation.
   Expected: separate debt, eligibility, coverage and booking; check actual policy.
5. An invoice email fails and the customer has no WhatsApp.
   Expected: verify spelling/delivery through authorized means, offer an available
   official alternative and distinguish sent from received and paid.
6. A short transcript ends after account identification.
   Expected: incomplete evidence, no allegation of intentional call termination.

These are fictionalized training inputs, not customer records. Policy-dependent
facts must come from the provider using the skill, not from the examples.
